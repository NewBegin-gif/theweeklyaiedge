My weekly routine as a systematic trader

When people hear I run a trading bot, I usually get the same picture back: someone behind three screens, price charts full of lines, tension, stress. That's true for a day trader, but not for my system. My week looks completely different. The system does the work, I keep watch on the markers. In this article, I'll walk you through a typical week in Saigon.

Monday morning: coffee and a quick check

My week starts quietly. I make coffee, open my laptop, and check the dashboard of my Bitvavo trading bot. It runs 24/7 on Replit, so there's nothing for me to start up. I check whether there were any transactions over the weekend, whether the logs show any errors, and whether the bot is still staying neatly within its rules. That doesn't take long. Then I close the tab and get on with my day: breakfast with the family, emails, content for my sites.

No tension, no urgency. The system has a plan, I just follow it.

Wednesday: reading the weekly report

Midweek I read the weekly report the bot sends me on Telegram: which trades it made and what the market did. It is not a show-off piece, more of a logbook. Now and then I check its numbers against the exchange itself, because a report can look right and still leave trades out.

Reading it also helps me reflect. Sometimes I spot a pattern in a weekly report that I'd otherwise miss. That's the value: discipline, not day trading.

Friday evening: the occasional rule tweak

Not every week, but sometimes I see something in the data that could be better. A trigger that reacts too eagerly, a stop-loss that's set just a bit too tight. Then I work out the change with Claude, push it to the bot’s code, and republish it on Replit myself, so I see every change before it goes live. I never do this in a rush, only when I'm certain it makes the system more stable. Improvements come from patterns, not from emotion.

The rest of the week I don't touch the code. It runs, I let it run.

The Edge Perspective

My week as a systematic trader is boring, and that's exactly the point. I'm not a day trader, I'm a former banker who won back his time by building a system that takes over the grunt work. Checking the dashboard, writing the weekly update, tweaking a rule when needed — that's it. I spend the rest of my time with my family, with my AI agent Victor who keeps my sites running, with living. The system does the work, I keep watch on the markers. That's the core of systematic trading: you build it well once, and then you let it do its job. No staring at screens, no stress. Just a plan and the discipline to follow it.

What I don't do: watch prices all day

I don't open a real-time price chart. I don't check every few hours whether the price of bitcoin has gone up or down. That would defeat the whole point. The moment you get emotionally invested in every swing, you lose the advantage of the system. The algorithm has no FOMO, no panic, no gambling impulses. It follows the rules I've set, and that's enough.

My routine is boring, and that's exactly what works. The week isn't about trading, it's about the family, the projects, life here in Saigon. The system is a tool, not a full-time job. That's how I wanted it, and that's how it runs now.
