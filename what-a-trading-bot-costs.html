What does it really cost to run your own trading bot?

When I first cobbled together my crypto trading bot with Replit's AI agent, one question came up more than any other: "What does something like that actually cost?" Makes sense, since the internet is full of stories about passive income and free automation. The truth is more nuanced, and I only really understand that now that I've been running my own system live on Bitvavo for months.

What I'm sharing below are my own actual expenses, no fabrications. Let me say upfront: this isn't financial advice, and there are no promises about profit. Just an honest breakdown from someone who'd rather spend his free time with his family in Saigon than with spreadsheets.

Hosting: where does your bot actually run?

A trading bot needs to be online 24/7, because markets never sleep. In my case, my system runs on a VPS at Hostinger, the same machine where my AI agent Victor lives, the one that maintains my websites. The cost of a VPS varies by provider and configuration, but the essence is: you need a stable environment with minimal downtime. Replit offers free tiers, but once you want to run continuously without interruptions, you end up either paying for a higher tier or moving to an external server.

One thing to note: your bot needs to be reliable. A missed order due to a server problem can end up costing more than a month of hosting fees.

Trading fees per transaction

This is where the biggest variable lies. Every time your bot places an order on an exchange like Bitvavo, you pay transaction fees. Those often drop as you trade more volume, but in the beginning you pay the base rate. That seems small per trade, but if your bot is active and, say, places orders daily, those costs add up.

My bot trades EUR/BTC and follows a weekly system with fixed percentages. That means fewer transactions than a high-frequency strategy, but I do feel the costs in my returns. The trick is: your profit margin needs to comfortably cover those transaction fees, otherwise the exchange eats your profit. Nobody likes talking about that, but it's a hard reality.

AI costs during the build

I had no developer background when I started. Replit's AI agent helped me write the code, but that also meant: lots of iterations, lots of prompts, lots of debugging. Some AI assistants charge per token or per session. Replit itself has limits on the free tier, and once you get serious about building your own bot, you'll run into those caps.

I didn't track exactly how much I spent during that phase, but it certainly wasn't zero. It felt like an investment in knowledge: every error message, every crashed run, every API connection failure was tuition. That comes with the territory, but it's not a free adventure.

The cost of learning, and the real price of experience

The biggest expense isn't on any invoice: time and mental energy. I spent a lot of time debugging my Bitvavo connection, setting up a dashboard that shows real-time data, and fine-tuning my strategy. Every time the bot did something I hadn't anticipated, it cost me evenings I would rather have spent with my wife and kids.

And then there are the emotional costs. The first time my bot made a losing trade, it felt more personal than a manual mistake would have. You built something, it runs autonomously, and yet you remain responsible. That's not a line item in euros, but it weighs heavily.

The Edge Perspective

I don't build bots because it's cheap, but because it gives me time back. The costs — hosting, transactions, AI tokens, the cost of learning — are real, and I'm not hiding them. But for me, the balance is positive: my system now runs with minimal manual work, and I get time back to spend with my family instead of with spreadsheets. That's the real ROI.

Want to start yourself? Don't count on free automation. Count on a learning curve, on costs you need to factor into your strategy, and on moments where you'll wonder whether it's worth it. For me, it was — but only because I was honest from the start about what it costs, in euros and in energy.

Disclaimer: this article shares my personal experience and is not financial advice. Crypto and traded products are risky — prices can fall hard, and a system that worked in the past is no guarantee for the future. Only trade with money you can afford to lose.
